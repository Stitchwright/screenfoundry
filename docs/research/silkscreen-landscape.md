# SilkFoundry can undercut a $650 stack

A free, MIT OR Apache-2.0 silkscreen prepress tool has a real opening, but the evidence for it is a price gap and a feature gap, not measured demand. The commercial market is a stack of separate products. A design host (Photoshop, Illustrator or CorelDRAW) is paired with a separator (Separation Studio NXT, UltraSeps, T-Seps, Separo) and a film RIP (AccuRIP Emerald, Wasatch SoftRIP SP). A 2022 estimate put Illustrator plus Separation Studio at up to **$778/yr**, and CorelDRAW plus Separation Studio at up to **$808/yr** ([YoPrint, May 2022](https://www.yoprint.com/do-screen-printers-need-color-separation-software/)). The open-source side has no maintained "full silkscreen studio". It has a tail of 0-2 star hobby repos, and the most feature-complete one (InkSplit) is GPL-3.0 with broken export ([GitHub](https://github.com/otacon239/inksplit)). Nothing found automates trapping or mesh recommendation, so SilkFoundry could lead there. The production domain is rule-heavy but only loosely standardised: mesh is roughly 4-5x lpi, 22.5 degrees is the common screen angle, and underbase choke is about 0.25-0.3 mm. These are practitioner rules of thumb, so every one must be a user-editable profile value. VectorCraft's command engine, StitchFoundry's embroidery precedent (a domain layer on the same engine) and a PhotoCraft raster sibling make a layered build natural. Licence hygiene matters: GPL, AGPL and imagequant 4.x are ideas-only, and no Adobe software may be run or measured. Evidence is thin throughout: vendor pages often returned 403 or 429, most user sentiment dates from 2015-2019, and several prices are unverified.

Shop-management software is a separate category priced at roughly $100-$250 or more a month, so it is not SilkFoundry's lane (see the pricing caveat below). Pricing carries two standing caveats. First, several figures conflict. For example, $399 is annual on one Freehand partner page and a flat rate on a retailer page, and Printavo's vendor page ($109/mo Lite) disagrees with Capterra ($99) ([Printavo](https://www.printavo.com/pricing); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/154421/Printavo/)). Second, nothing was found for current NXT-alone, UltraSeps, SoftRIP, ColorBurst, Ergosoft or Onyx prices. The honest competitive anchor is therefore "roughly $350-$400 for a separator plus a design licence of about $240-$270/yr", not a precise number.

Feature expectations are clearer than prices. Spot colour, simulated process, one-click underbase, choke/spread, channel merging to fit the press, RIP export, registration marks and halftones with set lpi and angles appear in every serious product. The same notes identify no product that advertises automatic trapping, mesh recommendation or mockups. This is an absence in the pages reached, not proof that no product has them. Platform coverage is also a differentiator. The found tools run on Windows and macOS only, and the Photoshop-hosted ones exclude Linux because Photoshop has no Linux build. That second point is an inference, since no source states it about these tools specifically. Adjacent verticals are a further fork: DTF is now widely run alongside screen printing, with **65.7%** of surveyed decorators offering it ([Graphics Pro, Sept 2026](https://cahaba-media-group.foleon.com/graphics-pro/september2026/apparel-decoration)), so DTF-ready export is worth planning as a secondary output.

User sentiment is weak evidence. The recurring theme is that every tool gets you about 90% of the way and hand-tweaking remains. A Sept/Oct 2025 trade column says printers still separate in Photoshop, Illustrator and CorelDRAW, and still tweak seps even with T-Seps or Separation Studio ([Screen Printing](https://editions.mydigitalpublication.com/article/Keep+Your+Seps+Sharp/5052977/854169/article.html)). That item was paraphrased from a search summary and not verified on the page. Everything else is 2015-2019 forum posts. No Reddit, Facebook-group or 2024-2026 YouTube content surfaced in any search. Manual Photoshop separation is reported at 10-15 minutes for simple jobs and 30-60 minutes for complex ones ([YoPrint](https://www.yoprint.com/do-screen-printers-need-color-separation-software/)). Market sizing is similarly thin: IBISWorld gives about **12,288** US custom screen printing businesses in 2025 ([IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/number-of-businesses/custom-screen-printing/4211), search summary). There is no data on schools, hobbyists, or artist printmakers.

## Open source offers ideas and permissive crates, not a product

No maintained open-source silkscreen studio exists. A GitHub search for "screen printing color separation" returned only six repos with 0-2 stars each, and searches for halftone/silkscreen separation and Inkscape spot-separation extensions returned none (search-bound claim; Python/PyPI and non-English communities were not exhaustively searched). InkSplit (GIMP plug-in) does palette-to-layers, white underbase and registration marks, but is GPL-3.0 and its README lists export as non-functional ([GitHub](https://github.com/otacon239/inksplit)). bwLabSP is the most sophisticated on halftone and ink modelling (OKLab, Kubelka-Munk simulation, AM and FM screening, configurable lpi and angles) but is browser-only, single-purpose, and has no standard licence, only "free for everyone to use, edit or entirely change it" ([GitHub](https://github.com/souheyell/bwLabSP)). The closest Rust analogue, kink80/cutter (egui plus CLI, k-means Lab quantisation, halftone screens, per-ink SVG with registration holes), targets laser stencils and states no licence ([GitHub](https://github.com/kink80/cutter)). Ghostscript's `tiffsep` device emits one TIFF per spot or process colour, up to 64 plates, but Ghostscript is AGPL ([Artifex release note](https://pdfa.org/artifex-software-is-pleased-to-announce-the-release-of-ghostscript-9-54-0/)). Trapping tooling was found nowhere in open source.

Licence handling follows a simple rule that matches the project's own non-negotiables. GPL and AGPL code is read for ideas and never copied or linked. A repo with no licence file is all-rights-reserved, so it is not reusable either, including color-separator, ScreenSep, cutter and bwLabSP. The table below lists what the notes verified for Rust building blocks. All entries come from crates.io metadata unless marked.

A clean stack follows from this: moxcms or lcms2 for colour management, palette for perceptual colour, kmeans_colors or in-house k-means for spot extraction, and in-house or dithr AM and FM halftoning. Median cut (Heckbert 1982), octree, k-means and NeuQuant are all published methods ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_quantization)), and rotated-screen maths can be written from public sources. Two caveats apply. The void-and-cluster and blue-noise-mask patents (US5912745, US5557709) appear to date from the 1990s and so are likely expired by 2026, but the notes did not verify their status, so check before shipping. And the project rule is absolute that no Adobe software may be launched, scripted or measured, so conformance and output comparisons against Illustrator or Photoshop are off the table. Behaviour comes from public documentation and from files users own and send. Where the notes cite Adobe's own tutorials (for example expand strokes and merge colours with Pathfinder in artwork prep), these are public documentation of behaviour only. SilkFoundry's own tests must be written from scratch from public specs such as DCS 2.0, TIFF and PDF.

## Production rules are practitioner heuristics, so they belong in profiles

The notes agree on structure while conflicting on numbers. The rules below should ship as defaults inside editable ink, mesh and press profiles, never as hard constants.

**Separation methods.** Simulated process, the most common method for photographic art, uses spot-colour channels of regular plastisol over a high-opacity base, with halftone dots, and routinely uses six to ten screens ([Ultraseps](https://ultraseps.com/?p=229); [Printable Press](https://www.printablepress.com/simulated-process-color-screen-printing-guide/)). Index colour reduces the image to a fixed ink palette with non-overlapping square pixels instead of dots. Spot colour needs only thresholded flat masks. A per-job `method` enum (spot, simulated_process, index, cmyk_process, monotone/duotone) should therefore drive defaults. Typical colour counts for true four-colour process, duotone and monotone were not sourced. Neither was any algorithm for palette selection or ink-mix simulation, since commercial tools keep theirs proprietary, so SilkFoundry's own approach (k-means or median cut in Lab, then merge near-duplicates) is a design choice to validate with printers.

**Mesh, lpi and dot limits.** Mesh is about 4-5x lpi (45 lpi on 205 or 255 mesh; 35 lpi on 160) ([Blue Cotton](https://www.bluecotton.com/blog/?p=8567); [ScreenPrinting.com](https://help.screenprinting.com/en-US/what-mesh-count-should-i-use-for-halftones-16556)). Garment lpi is typically 35-55, up to about 65 on fine mesh ([Printable Press](https://www.printablepress.com/how-to-print-halftones-screen-printing/)). Sources conflict on the ratio itself. Screen Printing magazine cites 3.5x or 4x as common, notes that weft count can differ 5-7% from the stated value, and says no ratio is truly predictive ([Screen Printing](https://screenprintingmag.com/?p=51)). Printable Press also gives a one-third-of-mesh rule in another passage. Sensible defaults are `lpi = mesh/4.5` with a warning band between mesh/5 (safe) and mesh/4 (aggressive). Textured goods need about 10 lpi less. The smallest reliably held dot is about **8%**, with a 4% anecdote on 305 mesh being unreliable ([Blue Cotton](https://www.bluecotton.com/blog/?p=8567); [Shirt Board](https://www.theshirtboard.com/index.php/topic,12015.15.html), snippet). Tints above about 70% fill in per a trade-site claim. No source gives a maximum dot or a numeric dot-gain curve for screen printing, so min and max dot and the gain curve must be per-ink-and-mesh profile parameters. The soft 90-95% upper cap in the gap-fill notes is an inference.

**Angles and dot shape.** Screen-print vendors converge on 22.5 degrees (22.5 and 67.5 for two-colour work) because 0/90 align with threads and 45 hits mesh knuckles ([ScreenPrinting.com](https://help.screenprinting.com/en-US/what-does-angle-mean-when-regarding-halftones-229249)). Printable Press, though, recommends 45 for single-colour work on light garments, so the sources conflict directly ([Printable Press](https://www.printablepress.com/how-to-print-halftones-screen-printing/)). Offset CMYK sets (US 15/75/0/45, European 15/45/0/75) are a forum-expert convention, not a standard ([Print Planet](https://printplanet.com/threads/screen-angles-why-are-they-15-45-75-and-90.10650/post-114308)). For three or more spot halftones, no sourced set exists. A moire checker that flags angle pairs closer than 30 degrees and angles aligned with the mesh is a reasonable feature. Its thresholds, plus the "at least 15 degrees from 0/45/90" preset, are my inference to validate with a printer. Dot shape has a source conflict too: elliptical dots join near 40%/60% per Wikipedia but near 50% per Printable Press, and the true value depends on the RIP's spot function ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halftone)).

**Underbase, trap, choke and registration.** Dark garments get a white underbase, flash cured (roughly 93-110 C surface for 3-8 s, ink and unit dependent) before colours go on, with a separate highlight white on top ([Lawson](https://lawsonsp.com/blogs/education-and-training/technical-screen-printing-tip-flash-cure-underbase-print); [Thrive](https://thrivescreenprinting.com/underbase-screen-printing)). Underbase choke clusters at about 0.25-0.3 mm (0.3 pt) in vector, and 1-2 px at 300 ppi or 4 px at 1200 ppi in raster ([The Shirt Board](https://theshirtboard.com/index.php/topic,21848.15.html)). Manual-press trapping runs 0.25-0.5 pt ([Printable Press](https://www.printablepress.com/screen-printing-registration-marks-how-to-use/)). One printer dissents, saying choking removes thin lines and preferring to expand the top colours ([Shirt Board](https://theshirtboard.com/index.php/topic,26784.5/wap2.html)), so the design should offer a thin-line-protect option. The trapping article's own point conversions are off by 2x (1/64 in is 1.125 pt, not 2.25), a reminder that vendor articles need checking ([Screen Printing](https://screenprintingmag.com/how-to-trap-artwork-for-screen-printing-4-essential-steps-to-avoid-gaps-and-misregistration)). Registration marks are crosshair-in-circle, 0.25-0.5 in, four at the corners outside the image (6-8 for photoreal) and on every screen including the underbase ([Printable Press](https://www.printablepress.com/screen-printing-registration-marks-how-to-use/)). No canonical print order was found, so order must be an editable list with flash steps, with underbase first as the only sourced constraint.

**Film, mesh by ink, CTS and DTF.** Film density (dMax) trades edge sharpness against dot gain on inkjet film, with tests suggesting about 2.5 or lower can work ([Kolormatrix](https://kolormatrix.com/blogs/news/controlling-film-density-for-better-image-quality); [BCH](https://bchtechnologies.com/blog/optimizing-inkjet-printing-for-screen-printing-films/)). Mesh depends on ink: glitter 24-86, thick plastisol and metallic around 86-125, standard plastisol 110-160, underbase 110-156, water-based and discharge 180-230 or above, fine halftones about 230, with sites conflicting on water-based (120-160 versus 230-280) ([Lawson](https://lawsonsp.com/blogs/education-and-training/screen-printing-mesh-count-production-guide); [Printable Press mesh guide](https://www.printablepress.com/screen-printing-mesh-count-guide/)). Ink tech sheets from Wilflex, Union, Murakami, Saati and Sefar were never read, so these remain defaults to be overridden. CTS machines consume 1-bit screen-ruled bitmaps at roughly 600 to over 2500 dpi ([Anatol](https://anatol.com/pre-press/screenmaker-computer-to-screen-system); [Screen Printing](https://screenprintingmag.com/?p=373)). DTF wants transparent PNG or TIFF at 300 dpi, with the RIP generating mirrored colour plus white ([BCH](https://bchtechnologies.com/blogs/questions-and-answers/do-you-need-rip-software-to-print-in-dtf-how-to-print-a-white-underbase-for-direct-to-film-printing)). DCS 2.0 (1993, revised 1995) carries spot plates, and Separation Studio NXT exports it ([DCS 2.0 spec](https://www.printtechnologies.org/standards/files/dcs20spec.pdf)). A forum thread says Illustrator dropped DCS 2.0 in CC 2018, which is unverified from the vendor ([Adobe community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/illustrator/dcs-2-0-no-longer-supported-in-illustrator-cc-2018/m-p/9400168)).

## Building SilkFoundry on the VectorCraft command engine

The strongest architectural fit is the StitchFoundry precedent: a domain layer of commands, tests and profiles on the existing engine, with the UI staying thin. The recommendation below is my design synthesis, not a sourced finding, and the only evidence about demand is the price and feature gap above.

**Where the work lives.** Vector-aware separation is a natural VectorCraft strength: spot, trap, choke and spread operate on paths with exact offsets (Separation Studio's own "Vector PDF engine" suggests incumbents see this as valuable). Raster-heavy work (simulated process, index colour, ink-mix simulation, 1-bit film bitmaps, DTF PNG) belongs in PhotoCraft or a shared pure-Rust crate with no egui dependency, consistent with the layering rule. Per the project rules, every user-visible behaviour is a command (for example `silk.separate`, `silk.underbase`, `silk.trap`, `silk.registrationMarks`, `silk.exportFilm`) with params docs, tests, and automatic reach through the control channel and MCP. MCP and CLI batch separation is a plausible differentiator that no found competitor offers, but that is an absence-of-evidence claim. A WASM build is another (InkSeps and Separo are the only browser competitors found), subject to the project rule never to break wasm. All parsers and importers (PSD with spot channels, DCS2, multichannel TIFF) must return `Result`, never panic, and extend the fuzz suites. Fonts and assets follow the repo's existing rules.

**Proposed MVP.** Phase 1 covers spot-colour separation of vector art: one plate per ink, ink library with Pantone-style links (library data must be original or licensed, never copied), underbase and highlight-white generation, per-pair choke/spread/trap, registration marks, overprint preview, and per-plate vector PDF plus 1-bit TIFF film export with mirror and density options. Phase 2 adds raster: index-colour and simulated-process separation with AM halftones (editable lpi, angle, dot shape, min and max dot, gain curve), mesh-aware warnings (lpi versus mesh, moire angle check, min-dot check), and press-order and flash planning. Phase 3 adds CTS 1-bit output, DTF gang sheets and white-layer export, DCS2 and multichannel PSD and TIFF interchange, FM and AM/FM hybrid screening, CMYK process presets, job sheets as CSV/JSON for shop-management integration (rather than competing with Printavo-class tools), and mockups only if demand appears (no source found for mockup features). Items deliberately deferred: ML separation (all vendor claims are unverified marketing) and ink-mixing physics beyond a documented Kubelka-Munk-style approximation.

**Roadmap and validation.** Ship phase 1 to a few shops and printmakers first, because the notes contain no reliable user research and the numeric defaults are soft. The 22.5-degree set, the 0.25-0.3 mm choke, the 8% minimum dot and the lpi-to-mesh ratio are the highest-leverage values to calibrate against real press tests. Do that with files that testers own and send, never by comparison against Adobe products. Keep each default tied to an ink, mesh and press profile so a shop can override it, which is also how to handle the source conflicts on angles, ratios and water-based mesh.

**Proposed data model.** The notes explicitly say no standard job schema exists, so this is design inference built from the rules above.

```
Job            { id, customer?, garment{colour, fabric}, press{stations, platen, flash_positions},
                 print_size, method: spot|simulated_process|index|cmyk_process|monotone,
                 art_source, status, notes }
InkLibrary     [ Ink { id, name, lab, rgb_preview, ink_type: plastisol|water|discharge|specialty|opaque_white,
                       opacity, pantone_link?, mesh_range, flash_required, role: underbase|highlight|colour|halftone } ]
Screen         { id, frame_size, mesh, tension?, ink_ref, station, registration_offset }
Plate          { id, ink_ref, screen_ref, lpi, angle, dot_shape, min_dot, max_dot, gain_curve,
                 order, mirrored, film_density_target, geometry: vector|bitmap_1bit,
                 trap_rules: [ { against_plate, kind: choke|spread|overprint, amount, unit, protect_thin_lines } ] }
OutputProfile  { id, device: film_inkjet|cts|dtf|pdf, dpi, linearization_per_lpi, mark_template }
PressPlan      { ordered stations: [ plate | flash | cure ] }
```

Defaults for these fields (lpi = mesh/4.5, min dot 8%, angle 22.5 and 67.5, underbase choke 0.25-0.3 mm, crosshair marks 0.25-0.5 in) are starting values drawn from the sources above and must be labelled soft in the UI and docs. Schema versioning and a legacy-compat path should follow the engine's existing file conventions.

## What the evidence cannot yet tell us

Several gaps limit confidence and should be closed before commitments are made. First, demand is unmeasured: no Reddit r/screenprinting, Facebook, or 2024-2026 YouTube material was found, no segment sizing exists for schools, hobbyists or poster printmakers, and survey bases (65.7%, 76%, 55-60%) are not comparable. Second, most vendor facts came from search snippets or pages that returned 403 or 429, including all of Solutions for Screen Printers, so features and prices for NXT, UltraSeps, SoftRIP SP, ColorBurst, Ergosoft and Onyx are weak or missing. Third, the numeric production rules rest on blogs and forums, and key tech sheets (Wilflex, Union, Murakami, Saati, Sefar, Ryonet) were not read. Fourth, source conflicts remain unresolved: Freehand $399 annual versus flat, 45 versus 22.5 degrees, mesh ratios of 3.5-5x, elliptical dot join points, and water-based mesh. Fifth, repo facts came from summarised GitHub pages, licence files were mostly not read verbatim, and last-commit dates are unknown. Sixth, no independent output comparison of any separator or RIP exists, so claims of "best in class" are marketing. Seventh, patent status for void-and-cluster, the Little CMS licence, and the ImageMagick, GraphicsMagick and Gutenprint licences are unchecked. Finally, vinyl and stencil crossover, PDF/X-4 spot handling, print-order rules, frame image-area limits and ink deposit limits were not found.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "can a free tool match the incumbents?" to "which slice is least served?" Incumbents compete on automated photographic separation, and users still say they hand-tweak the results. The unserved slices are vector-native trapping and mesh-aware profile checking, plus cross-platform, scriptable, no-dongle operation. Those are also exactly where VectorCraft's path geometry, command engine and MCP surface are strongest. A vector-first phase 1 therefore plays to the codebase's strengths and defers the contested ML-style raster separation until printer-validated defaults exist.

The practical implication is that SilkFoundry's moat is a documented, editable rules layer (mesh, lpi, angle, dot limits, choke, flash and order) wrapped around a commodity-licence stack, not secret algorithms. Because the numbers are soft and partly contradictory, calibrating them with willing print shops, using their own files and never Adobe software, is the single most valuable next step, ahead of any feature work.
